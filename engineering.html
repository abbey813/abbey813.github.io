My second home was the Froom (Fringe-room), where we built our buggies. The froom has a belt sander, bandsaw, woodworking machines, and various hand tools. So when I wanted to machine an aluminum brake or mounting tab for a robotic conveyor belt, I went to Techspark: my third home. Techspark is CMU's machine shop, where I made anything my heart desired. I took just about every class offered: Intro to Machining, CNC Programming, Woodworking I, and Jewelry Fundamentals.

Between jewelry and buggy, I think the dremel is just about the most essential tool for an artistic engineer. And I suppose that's what I like about engineering: sure, there's rules you have to follow and certain optimized techniques, but for much of the time there's some wiggle room for creativity.

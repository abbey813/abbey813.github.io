Buggy is one of CMU's longest-running traditions. Eleven teams compete to race an aerodynamic, engineless vehicle with a driver inside lying stomach-down, arms forward. It is most commonly built of carbon fiber and has three wheels, but other than that, teams differentiate themselves with their custom steering+braking systems, fairing shape, wheels, and weight-saving techniques. Each buggy must pass rigorous safety checks which include driver harness attachment points, shell structural integrity, and an adequate field of vision for the driver. More info can be found in this CMU news article, or on the Buggy Alumni Association website!

I started my journey with buggy when I was initially recruited as a driver. Teams look for short drivers so they can build smaller buggies and save mass - so at 5'0", I was the perfect fit. I adored driving buggies for the ardrenaline rush, but I was immediately more captivated by the engineering behind it. I attended build sessions and started learning the build process, which entailed carbon fiber wet layups, building fairings from polyeurethane expanding flotation foam, hardware maintenance & brake tuning, and the magic of epoxy. Throughout the year, I felt myself diving deeper and deeper into the world of buggy and mechanical engineering. I had a google doc of things that worked, and things that didn't: all to keep in mind for the following year when we built the next buggy (Fringe Racing is the only team that builds a new one every year).

head mechanic again. design changes, more analysis, upgraded CAD

One of the places with the largest room for improvement was the wheels: all it took was a sticky bearing or unbalanced center of mass to throw off the entire race. We coordinated with our wheel manufacturer to purchase softer wheels, which traded durability for speed. We also conducted testing to find the ideal temperature to heat our wheels to, as well as a new method for wheel heating (an air fryer!). Additionally, I ordered reflective foil tape to put on the inside of the wheel well and fairing cover. As a driver, I noticed the inside of the buggy getting warm right next to the wheels and figured the heat was conducting through the carbon fiber, which was something no other mechanic would have known. It proved effective in our tests :)

My second home was the Froom (Fringe-room), where we built our buggies. The froom has a belt sander, bandsaw, woodworking machines, and various hand tools. Coursework at CMU
  • 24-721: Advanced Thermodynamics
    • A molecular-level understaning of key thermodynamic quantities like heat, work, free energy and entropy. Concepts are applied in understanding several important engineering applications, such as electron and thermal transport.
  • 24-292: Renewable Energy Engineering
    • Introduction to various renewable energy systems, including: climate change and carbon sequestration, analysis of renewable energy systems such as solar photovoltaic, wind, hydropower, wave energy, biomass energy, geothermal, and hydrogen based fuel cells. In addition, transitional energy systems such as nuclear power and advanced combined cycles will be introduced.
  • 24-626: Air Quality Engineering
    • The course provides an introduction to the processes that control atmospheric pollutants, and the use of mass balance models to predict pollutant concentrations. Topics include emission rates, atmospheric dispersion, chemistry, and deposition. Uses atmospheric concepts and meteorology including circulation, stability, and boundary layer turbulence. Special focus is given to tropospheric ozone and particulate matter.
  • 16-865: Space Robotics
    • The course conveys technologies of space robotics, especially development of planetary robot sensing, computing, thermal management, communications, and operator interaction in addition to mobility, mechanics, electronics, and navigation. Additionally, space systems considerations address challenges of low mass, space environment, reliability, testing and risk reduction. Lectures convey topical content, and labs constitute subteam work sessions.
Coursework at Stanford
  • AA-210.A: Fundamentals of Compressible Flow
    • In this course, gain a physical understanding of the phenomena and analytical techniques. Topics include: fluid flow and thermodynamics of gases, control volumes, vector calculus, equations of motion, gas dynamics of nozzles, steady and unsteady waves, oblique shocks, Prandtl-Meyer expansion, linearized potential flow, thin airfoil theory
  • ME-362.A: Physical Gas Dynamics
    • Concepts and techniques for description of high-temperature and chemically reacting gases from a molecular point of view. Introductory kinetic theory, chemical thermodynamics, and statistical mechanics as applied to properties of gases and gas mixtures. Transport and thermodynamic properties, law of mass action, and equilibrium chemical composition. Maxwellian and Boltzmann distributions of velocity and molecular energy. Examples and applications from areas of current interest such as combustion and materials processing.
  • CS-238: Decision Making under Uncertainty
    • This course introduces decision making under uncertainty from a computational perspective. Following an introduction to probabilistic models and decision theory, the course covers stochastic dynamics, model uncertainty, and imperfect state information. Topics include Bayesian networks, influence diagrams, dynamic programming, reinforcement learning, and partially observable Markov decision processes. Applications cover air traffic control, aviation surveillance systems, and robotic planetary exploration.
  • AA-256: Mechanics of Composites
    • Fiber reinforced composite structures. Stress, strains and deformations in composite laminated plates and shells. In-plane and out-of-plane coupling effects. Manufacturing processes and residual stresses. Bending and torsional rigidities of composite tubes, beams, and columns. Failure modes and failure criteria in composites and hole-size effects on composite structures. Class involves also fabrication and testing of composite I-beams.
